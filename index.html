1993

The year women had to be included in federally funded clinical research.

More than 30 years later, many medicines are still studied mostly in men, and pregnant women are left out almost entirely.

Your experience fills that gap.

How it works

Four steps from your own experience to research women can use.

  1. 1

    Make it about you

    Join the study with a one-page survey about your body, your birth control, your medicines and your stage of life.

  2. 2

    Pick your medicine

    Look up a common medicine and see who it was studied on, who was left out, and which side effects women report more.

  3. 3

    See the stats

    Every report is counted, so you can see how common a side effect really is for women on your mix, by trimester if you're pregnant.

  4. 4

    Research Assistant

    Ask a question in plain words. It summarizes what the research and members say, and points you to the forum threads where your mix is being discussed.

Where this comes from

Side-effect differences come from AwareDX, a machine-learning analysis of FDA reports by Payal Chandak and Nicholas Tatonetti at Columbia University. Drug-level differences come from Zucker and Prendergast at UC Berkeley. invisiblewomen is not affiliated with either university.

    Pregnancy

    Pregnant women have long been kept out of drug trials as a “vulnerable population.” The official registries are mostly run by the companies that make each medicine. Ours is open: every pregnant member's report is counted here, for everyone to see.
